Accessibility Decision Lab

Evaluate three openly licensed budgeting tools and recommend one for independent online learning.

Your assignment · approximately 4–6 minutes

Choose a budgeting activity

Your team is developing an asynchronous personal finance course. Students must create and adjust a monthly budget independently. All three candidate resources are fictional, openly licensed under CC BY 4.0, and broadly support the objective. Your decision should prioritize whether students with different access needs can complete the essential task without assistance.

Review each profile, record a WCAG-based evaluation, then recommend a resource using two pieces of evidence. This is practice, not a graded quiz; you can revise and retry.

Evaluation criteria: the four WCAG principles
Perceivable
Information and feedback are available in forms learners can perceive, including text equivalents and sufficient contrast.
Operable
Every essential control works with a keyboard, with logical focus order and visible focus.
Understandable
Instructions, labels, and error recovery are clear and predictable.
Robust
Controls and updates expose meaningful names, roles, and states to assistive technologies.

Your goal: Recommend the resource that best enables all learners to complete the monthly budgeting activity independently. Consider how accessibility barriers might affect essential tasks.

1. Examine the candidate resources

Select each card to examine its instructional features and accessibility testing evidence.

2. Record your accessibility evaluations

For each candidate, select the assessment that best matches the impact of the evidence on independent task completion. Use the WCAG criteria above; the profile observations are the evidence.

3. Make a professional recommendation

Which resource would you select?
Choose exactly two evidence-based reasons supporting your recommendation.

Select the two strongest considerations, not merely appealing features.

This practice activity is ungraded. You can review the feedback and revise your responses at any time. Your optional reflection is for self-assessment and is not automatically scored.

4. Review Your Decision

Example: How an Instructional Designer Might Explain This Decision

Example Recommendation

I recommend Budget Builder C because learners can complete the entire budgeting activity using a keyboard, and screen readers can announce changes to budget totals.

Budget Canvas A requires dragging items with a mouse, which prevents some learners from completing an essential task. Monthly Money Map B allows keyboard navigation, but its updated totals are not consistently announced to screen-reader users.

Although Budget Builder C has a denser layout that may take time to learn, it provides the strongest overall accessibility support for learners completing the budgeting activity independently.
